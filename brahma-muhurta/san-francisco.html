What is Brahma Muhurta?

Brahma Muhurta is the sacred pre-dawn window traditionally reserved for meditation, yoga, and spiritual practice. The name means "the time of Brahma" or "the hour of the Creator." It begins roughly 1 hour 36 minutes before sunrise and lasts about 48 minutes — one muhurta in the traditional division of the day into thirty muhurtas of 48 minutes each.

San Francisco Bay Area is home to one of the densest Indian-American populations in the country, across the wider Bay Area and Silicon Valley, and many here keep the practice of rising in Brahma Muhurta for meditation and yoga — a tradition carried from India and adapted to life in California.

Why the Time Changes Each Day in San Francisco Bay Area

There is no single fixed Brahma Muhurta time in San Francisco Bay Area. At this mid-latitude, sunrise times swing noticeably between summer and winter, so the Brahma Muhurta window moves with them. Across the year, the Brahma Muhurta time in San Francisco Bay Area can move noticeably — often by well over an hour. This is why the times above are calculated from San Francisco Bay Area's real sunrise rather than given as a fixed clock time.

Why Practise in Brahma Muhurta?

The window is prized because the mind is naturally calm after sleep, the city is quiet before the day begins, and tradition holds that clarity (sattva) is at its peak. For anyone in San Francisco Bay Area looking to begin a meditation or yoga practice, the Brahma Muhurta window offers the stillest, most undisturbed time of day.
