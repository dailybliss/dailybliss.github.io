Mantra Guide

Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya: Meaning, Benefits & How to Chant

The Dwadashakshari — the twelve-syllable mantra of Vishnu, and the chief of the Vaishnava mantras.

Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya is one of the most revered mantras in the Vaishnava tradition — a salutation to Lord Vishnu in his form as Vasudeva (Krishna). Known as the Dwadashakshari, the "twelve-syllable mantra," it is celebrated across the Bhagavata Purana, the Vishnu Purana, and the Mahabharata, and is traditionally regarded as a mukti mantra — a formula for liberation. This guide covers what the mantra means word by word, its origin, its benefits, and how to chant it as part of a daily practice.

The Vishnu Mantra

In Sanskrit (Devanagari):

ॐ नमो भगवते वासुदेवाय

In transliteration:

Oṁ Namo Bhagavate Vāsudevāya

Pronounced roughly: Om Nah-mo Bhuh-guh-vuh-tay Vah-soo-day-vah-ya. Counting from "Na," the mantra contains exactly twelve syllables — Na-mo-Bha-ga-va-te-Vā-su-de-vā-ya — which is why it is called the Dwadashakshari (dwadasha = twelve, akshara = syllable).

Word-by-Word Meaning

  • Om (ॐ) — the primordial sound of the universe, the seed from which all mantras arise, denoting the Supreme Infinite Spirit.
  • Namo (नमो) — "I bow," "salutations," "reverence." An expression of humble surrender.
  • Bhagavate (भगवते) — "to the Lord," "to the divine one" — the Bhagavan, the supremely powerful, glorious, and merciful.
  • Vāsudevāya (वासुदेवाय) — "to Vasudeva." On one level this names Krishna as the son of Vasudeva; on a deeper level, Vasudeva is interpreted as "the one who dwells (vāsa) in all beings" — the divine present within everything and everyone.

Put together, the mantra means: "Om, I bow to Lord Vasudeva — the divine that dwells in all beings." It is at once a surrender to Vishnu/Krishna and a recognition that the same divine presence pervades all of existence.

Who Is Vasudeva?

Vasudeva is a name of Krishna, who in the Vaishnava tradition is understood as a full incarnation (avatar) of Vishnu — the preserver among the great trinity. In the Bhagavad Gita, Arjuna addresses Krishna by this name. The mantra therefore honours the Supreme in his approachable, personal form: the divine that both transcends creation and lives within every heart.

The tantric text Sharada Tilaka declares Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya "the chief among Vaishnava mantras." In the Vaishnava Upanishads it is described as inscribed upon Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra — his discus — and in the Bhagavata Purana it is presented as a supreme formula for devotion and liberation.

Origin in the Scriptures

The mantra appears across the foundational texts of the tradition. In the Bhagavata Purana (Shrimad Bhagavatam), it is held as an ultimate mantra of devotion. The Vishnu Purana contains it. In the Mahabharata, the young prince Dhruva is said to have chanted this mantra during his intense penance — a story often retold as an example of the mantra's power to fulfil sincere devotion. Its presence across Purana, Upanishad, and epic gives it an unusually broad scriptural foundation.

Benefits of Chanting Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya

The mantra is chanted both for its devotional purpose and for the benefits of steady mantra practice. Practitioners and traditional teaching describe:

  • Cultivating devotion (bhakti) — the mantra's primary purpose is to open the heart in love and surrender to the divine, deepening one's sense of connection.
  • A path toward liberation — traditionally regarded as a mukti mantra, a formula chanted for spiritual freedom and release from worldly attachment.
  • Inner peace and protection — devotees chant it for a sense of divine shelter, comfort in difficulty, and a settled, protected mind.
  • Calm and clarity — like all steady chanting, the rhythmic repetition calms the nervous system, eases anxiety, and clears mental agitation.
  • Steadiness in practice — a resonant, flowing mantra well suited to building a daily japa habit, especially for those drawn to the devotional (Bhakti) path.

How to Chant the Mantra

  1. Sit comfortably with a straight spine, eyes closed. If possible, face east or towards an image of Krishna or Vishnu.
  2. Take a few slow breaths to settle the mind and gently set your intention of devotion.
  3. Begin chanting Oṁ Namo Bhagavate Vāsudevāya at a steady, unhurried pace — aloud, whispered, or silently in the mind.
  4. Let each repetition flow smoothly into the next, keeping attention gently on the sound and its meaning.
  5. Chant a fixed number of repetitions — traditionally 108, counted on a mala or with a counter — or for a set period of time.
  6. When finished, rest quietly for a moment in the settled, devotional state the practice creates.

How Many Times and When

The traditional count is 108 repetitions — one full round of a japa mala. Many devotees chant it each morning; some undertake longer commitments of several rounds daily, particularly on Ekadashi and other days sacred to Vishnu.

The most auspicious time to chant is the pre-dawn window of Brahma Muhurta, when the mind is naturally quiet. Thursday is traditionally associated with Vishnu. That said, the mantra can be chanted at any time — many devotees repeat it silently throughout the day as a continuous remembrance of the divine.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya mean?

It means "Om, I bow to Lord Vasudeva — the divine that dwells in all beings." It is a salutation to Lord Vishnu in his form as Vasudeva (Krishna). "Vasudeva" names Krishna as the son of Vasudeva and also means the one who lives within all beings.

Why is it called the Dwadashakshari mantra?

"Dwadashakshari" means "twelve-syllable" (dwadasha = twelve, akshara = syllable). Counting from "Na," the mantra has exactly twelve syllables: Na-mo-Bha-ga-va-te-Vā-su-de-vā-ya. The Sharada Tilaka calls it the chief among Vaishnava mantras.

Is this a mantra for Krishna or Vishnu?

Both. Vasudeva is a name of Krishna, who in the Vaishnava tradition is understood as a full incarnation of Vishnu. The mantra honours the Supreme in his personal, approachable form, so it is chanted by devotees of Krishna and of Vishnu alike.

How many times should I chant it?

The traditional count is 108 repetitions (one round of a mala), often each morning. Longer daily commitments of several rounds are common among devotees, especially on Ekadashi. Consistency matters more than the number.

Can I chant it silently?

Yes. Silent mental repetition (manasa japa) is widely practised and considered highly effective, particularly as a continuous remembrance during meditation or daily activity. Beginners often find chanting aloud or whispered helps maintain focus.

Do I need initiation to chant this mantra?

Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya is a widely and openly chanted mantra that anyone can practise with sincerity and respect. While some traditions offer formal initiation (diksha), no initiation is required to begin chanting it devotionally.
